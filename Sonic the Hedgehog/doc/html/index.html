The Sonic 1 PC Handbook

Sonic 1 PC is Sonic the Hedgehog (1991, Mega Drive/Genesis) as a native program: the same game, in a window with a menu bar, a picture that can change shape, modern audio and a set of tools for looking inside it. This handbook tells you how to play it, what is waiting for you in every zone and special stage, how to set up the picture, the sound and the controls, and what the secrets are.

Crush Dr. Robotnik!

South Island has a villain, and he has a plan. Dr. Ivo Robotnik is rounding up the island's animals, sealing them inside robot bodies and combing every zone for the six Chaos Emeralds. The only one fast enough to stop him is Sonic: a hedgehog with a taste for speed, a spiky attitude and a very good pair of sneakers.

Your job is to run, roll and spin through six zones, smash Robotnik's robots (the badniks) to set the animals free, grab rings to stay in one piece, and face the doctor himself at the end of each zone. Find the secret special stages and you may get your hands on the Chaos Emeralds as well. Ready? Be quick, be bold, be Sonic.

The title screen

This is the real 1991 game, running as a native program on your computer. Its rules, its physics and its quirks are the cartridge's, kept as they were unless there was a good reason to change them. What is new is everything around the game:

  • The picture can be the original shape or one of four others, in a window or full screen ().

  • Music and sound effects have separate volumes, and every sound chip channel can be muted ().

  • The keyboard and any gamepad can be set up the way you like, including how far a worn stick has to move before it counts ().

  • Sonic can charge a spin dash, the ending and the credits are complete, and so is the continue screen.

  • A few of the original's bugs are fixed: you cannot walk-jump off a monitor or a wall with the wrong pose any more, TIME flashes at nine minutes whether or not you have rings (the later games did that too), and the game-over music plays.

  • A debug mode opens windows onto the game's video memory, sound chips, objects and variables, with a console, a demo recorder and the SMPS Inspector ().

New to the game? Read on from . Know it by heart? Jump to for the picture settings or for the secrets.

Starting Up

Start the game

Run Sonic. The window opens on the SEGA screen, then the title screen. Press Return (or Start on a gamepad) to begin a new game in Green Hill Zone. Leave the title screen alone for a few moments and a demonstration game plays; press Start to return to the title, and Start again to begin.

The main window

The menu bar sits inside the window. File, Audio, Settings and Help are always there; View and Tools appear while the debug tools are available (). The right-hand side of the status bar lists the tools that are running, so that none of them goes unnoticed. F1 opens this handbook.

The buttons can be changed in Settings → Configure Sonic the Hedgehog.... Click a button in the table, then press the key (or, in the gamepad column, the gamepad button) you want; Escape cancels. Every Mega Drive button has two keyboard keys, and a key can drive only one button (assigning it elsewhere takes it away from the old one). The D-pad and left stick always move Sonic on a gamepad. F11 and the backtick key are reserved. Restore Defaults puts back the table above. The choices are saved with the other settings.

The controls settings

The Gamepad tab of the same window sets how far a stick has to move before it counts: a dead zone for the left stick, which moves Sonic, and one for the right stick, which only the debug tools use. A stick that is worn and drifts can make Sonic creep along on its own; raise its dead zone until the dot in the picture stays inside the shaded square while the stick is at rest. This does what Steam Input's dead zone would, without needing it. Each stick has a picture and a readout showing where it is as you move it, as a percentage of its travel, and whether that counts as a push or is inside the dead zone.

The dead zone has two shapes. A Box judges each direction on its own: the stick has to pass the line on that axis. A Ring judges the stick's overall push, in any direction: drift that sits a little off to one side stays ignored, and once the stick is pushed past the ring, the push is read as one of eight directions. The shaded area in the picture changes with the choice.

The gamepad settings

What the buttons do:

D-pad

Left and right move Sonic, and holding a direction makes him speed up. Standing still, press up or down to look at the top or bottom of the screen (it will not scroll further than the level goes). Pressing down while you run rolls him into a ball.

Button A, B or C

Any of the three makes Sonic jump; hold it for a higher jump. A jump is a spin attack too, so it is also how you land on a badnik.

Start

Starts the game, and pauses it. Press it again to carry on. While the game is paused, holding B or pressing C steps a single frame.

Sonic's super stunts

  • Press down while Sonic is running to roll up into the spin attack: a rolling Sonic bumps badniks out of the way instead of getting hurt by them.

  • Press a jump button while rolling to jump with the spin attack still on.

  • Standing still, press down to duck.

The spin dash

Duck with Down, then press a jump button to start charging a spin dash. Each further press winds it up more and raises the pitch of the revving sound. Release Down to shoot off. (This is Sonic 2's move, added here: the cartridge's Sonic cannot do it.)

The secret level select

The level select is hidden. At the title screen press Up, Down, Left, Right (a ring sound tells you it worked), then hold A and press Start.

The level select

Move the highlight with Up and Down and choose a row with any of A, B, C or Start. The two last rows are the Special Stage and the sound test: on SOUND SELECT, Left and Right change the number and a button plays that sound. The highlighted row and the sound number are remembered until you quit the game.

A level chosen from the level select keeps your special stage number and your emeralds, just like entering a level through a giant ring, and the special stage numbers go on from where they were.

Playing the Game

Reach for the Rings!

The island has six zones, and every zone has three acts. Dash to the signpost at the end of an act to finish it; the third act of each zone ends in a fight with Dr. Robotnik instead. Along the way you will dodge traps and robots, so keep your eyes open for rings.

Rings are your armour. While you hold any, something that hurts Sonic only knocks them all out of his hands (you can chase some of them down and pick them up again). With none left, the next hit costs a life. A spin attack protects you from badniks but not from things that crush you, and nothing protects you from falling down a pit or drowning. Every 100 rings earn a life, and so does every 50,000 points.

Green Hill Zone

Keeping count

The counters on the screen tell you how you are doing:

  1. SCORE: your score so far.

  2. TIME: how long the act has taken. You have ten minutes for each act: go over, and TIME OVER costs you a life. It is game time, not clock time, so it stops while the game is paused, and TIME starts flashing once nine minutes have passed.

  3. RINGS: the rings you hold. The word flashes when you have none.

  4. SONIC ×: the lives you have left, in the bottom left corner (the top right corner when the picture is one of the wider shapes).

Lampposts

A lamppost

Lampposts stand in every zone. Run past one and it remembers where you got to, along with your score and time. If you lose a life you restart from the last lamppost you touched, without your rings but with that score and time (a TIME OVER clears the remembered time, so you cannot get stuck restarting with the clock almost out).

Zip Through the Zones!

Six zones, three acts each. Every zone has its own look, its own traps and its own cast of badniks, and the third act ends with a boss: Dr. Robotnik at the controls of a different machine each time. The zones come in this order.

1. Green Hill Zone

Green Hill Zone

Sun, palm trees and waterfalls: the friendliest place on the island, and the best one to learn the ropes in. Run around the loops, tumble through tunnels, and look for the faster, higher routes above the main path.

  • Terrain: loops and tunnels you can take at full speed, wooden bridges that sag under Sonic, swinging platforms, ledges that crumble a moment after you land on them, and rock walls you can smash with a spin attack.

  • Hazards: plenty of spikes (jump them, never land on them) and springs that throw you a long way.

  • Badniks: Moto Bug, Crabmeat, Buzz Bomber, Chopper and Newtron.

  • Boss (Act 3): Robotnik flies back and forth with a heavy checkered ball swinging beneath his machine. Dodge the ball and jump on the machine; it takes eight hits.

2. Marble Zone

Marble Zone

Ancient ruins, built over pools of lava. The route goes over, under and through the stonework, so slow down and think: this zone is more puzzle than sprint.

  • Terrain: green stone blocks you can push (shove one onto the lava and it floats along for a while before it sinks), bricks that wobble above the lava and drop when you come near, pillars that rise when you stand on a switch, grassy slabs, and blocks that break when you hit them.

  • Hazards: lava itself (touching it hurts), balls of fire that jump out of it, geysers, heavy weights hung on chains that pound the floor, spikes, and in places a wall of lava that creeps after you.

  • Badniks: Buzz Bomber, Batbrain and Caterkiller.

  • Boss (Act 3): Robotnik cruises above the lava in a heated machine and drops fire onto the platforms below. Keep out from under it and hit it whenever it comes within reach.

3. Spring Yard Zone

Spring Yard Zone

A neon city that plays like a pinball table. Round bumpers fling Sonic every which way, springs fire him across the screen, and there is more of the zone off the main path than on it. Look out for the giant rings: this zone hides the most.

  • Terrain: bumpers, springs by the dozen, blocks that move in set patterns, platforms and spinning searchlights in the background.

  • Hazards: big spiked balls, balls swinging on chains, and spikes.

  • Badniks: Crabmeat, Buzz Bomber, Roller and Spikes (Yadrin).

  • Boss (Act 3): Robotnik's machine carries a spiked claw that comes down onto the blocks in the arena floor, lifts one, shakes it and lets it go. Keep out from under a falling block and strike the machine while the claw is busy.

4. Labyrinth Zone

Labyrinth Zone

A flooded maze of carved stone. Much of it is under water, where Sonic moves slowly and, more to the point, cannot breathe. Learn the water first, the rest comes after.

  • Air: Sonic can hold his breath for about half a minute. Warning chimes sound as it runs down, and in the last twelve seconds the music changes and numbers count 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0 up from Sonic's head. If you reach zero he drowns. Air bubbles drifting up from vents give you a gulp of air at once: stand in one and breathe.

  • Terrain: blocks that sink or rise when you stand on them, cork blocks that bob on the water, platforms riding on rope-and-wheel conveyors (they loop round, carrying you with them), doors that switches open, flap doors that swing on a timer, waterfalls and rapids.

  • Hazards: harpoons that stab out of the walls on a timer, spikes, spiked balls, and stone gargoyle heads that spit fire.

  • Badniks: Jaws, Burrobot and Orbinaut.

  • Boss (Act 3): there is no arena. Robotnik flies up a long shaft and you chase him, hitting him as you climb. He pauses at the top until you catch up, then escapes to the right.

5. Star Light Zone

Star Light Zone

A highway in the sky, thick with moving parts and Robotnik's bombs. It is full of things that move you about, and the trick is to let them.

  • Terrain: seesaws that launch you when something heavy lands on the other end, lifts, platforms that circle on arms, staircases whose blocks step out of the wall, big fans that blow you upward, springs, floors that fall away behind you, and girders that pass in front of the picture.

  • Badniks: Bomb and Orbinaut.

  • Boss (Act 3): Robotnik drops exploding spiked balls onto the seesaws. Use a seesaw to launch yourself at his machine, and keep clear of the balls when they go off.

6. Scrap Brain Zone

Scrap Brain Zone

Robotnik's own factory, one step from his hideout, and it holds every trap he could build. The factory is the toughest of the six zones.

Note:

Scrap Brain Zone was not always called that. During development it was Clock Work Zone, and the font of the title card had no W, so the card read CLOCK ORK ZONE.

  • Terrain: conveyor belts and spinning discs that carry you, floors that spin or vanish, doors, teleporters that send you to another part of the factory, and moving blocks.

  • Hazards: circular saws, flamethrowers, electric zappers, stompers, spiked balls, and spikes.

  • Badniks: Ball Hog, Caterkiller, Bomb and Orbinaut. Act 3 is flooded and brings the water back, with Jaws and Burrobot.

Beat the three acts and you reach Final Zone, where Robotnik waits in a private arena. He hides inside cylinders and fires plasma balls at you, so keep moving. Defeat him there and the ending begins ().

The Secret Zone: Special Stages

Rings are good for more than armour. Carry 50 or more to a giant ring and it glitters and lets you jump in: you are whisked away to a special stage, a secret place where Sonic is a rolling ball inside a big, slowly rotating maze, with a Chaos Emerald hidden somewhere in it. There are six special stages, one for each emerald. Giant rings are hidden in some acts of every zone except Scrap Brain; once you hold all six emeralds, they stop appearing.

A special stage

How it works

  • The maze turns steadily around Sonic. Steer with left and right, jump with A, B or C, and use the blocks to bounce your way through. There is no clock in here: take your time.

  • The stage is a trap, and there are exactly two ways out. Touch the Chaos Emerald to win it, or touch a GOAL block to leave without it (that is how you fail a stage). Either way you go back to the zone you came from.

  • Stages come in turn: if you fail, the next attempt is the next stage, and stages whose emerald you already hold are skipped.

  • Rings in the maze count too. Leave with 50 or more rings and the first time you do it you earn a continue (). The results screen adds 100 points for every ring you have.

  • Look out for ghost blocks: parts of the wall that you can pass through once, but which turn into solid red-and-white blocks behind you. They can trap you.

  • Six emeralds, six colours: blue, yellow, pink, green, red and white. Collect all six and the ending changes ().

Hints

  • The maze starts slow. Do not touch an UP block until you are ready for it: the faster maze gives you less time to line up a jump.

  • Know where the GOAL blocks are before you start bouncing around: they are easy to hit by accident, and they end the stage.

  • An UP block you have used turns into a DOWN block (and the other way round), so you can always bring the speed back.

  • An R block reverses the turning, which turns a bad approach into a good one: use it to change your angle.

  • Glass blocks give way after a few touches, changing colour as they weaken: hit one over and over to open a shortcut.

Game Over, Continues and Score

You start with three lives. Lose them all and the game is over: GAME OVER sounds its jingle and you are sent back to the SEGA screen, unless you have a continue.

Continues

Collecting 50 rings in a special stage earns a continue. When you lose your last life and have one, the CONTINUE screen appears: Sonic lies on the floor while a countdown runs from ten. The little Sonics above the number show how many continues you have (the one you are about to use is not shown). Press Start before the countdown reaches zero and Sonic gets up and runs off, and you start the act again with three lives; your rings, time and score begin from nothing. If the countdown runs out the game is over and returns to the SEGA screen.

The continue screen

Scoring

Each badnik you smash is worth at least 100 points, and beating Dr. Robotnik at the end of a zone is worth 1,000. When you finish an act, two bonuses are added to your score: a Time Bonus, bigger the faster you were, and a Ring Bonus, 100 points for every ring you still have. Every 50,000 points gives you an extra life.

The Ending and the Credits

Defeat Dr. Robotnik in the Final Zone and the ending begins: Sonic runs back to the animals in Green Hill. With all six Chaos Emeralds he holds them up, they spin, the screen flashes white and flowers bloom everywhere; without them he simply leaps at the screen. Either way the SONIC THE HEDGEHOG logo slides in and the credits follow.

The ending
The logo

If you wrecked Dr. Robotnik's ship as he fled the Final Zone, you can see it fall in the distant background of the ending while Sonic runs: a long-forgotten graphic that the original game loaded and never used. It appears with either ending.

The credits

The credits show a page of staff for two seconds and then play a short demonstration of one of the zones (Green Hill, Marble, Spring Yard, Labyrinth, Star Light, Scrap Brain, twice, and Green Hill again) before the next page.

A credits page

The credits end on one of two screens: END if Sonic has all six Chaos Emeralds, with Robotnik raging, or TRY AGAIN if not, with Robotnik juggling the emeralds you did not find. Start (or thirty seconds) returns to the SEGA screen.

Pick Your Picture: Resolution and Full Screen

The original game draws a 320 × 224 picture (an aspect ratio of 10:7) that a television stretches to fit. Sonic 1 PC can draw the picture in other shapes, adding extra screen where the game has room for it rather than stretching what is there.

The Resolution menu

Open File → Resolution and choose the shape of the picture.

In a window, choosing a resolution also resizes the window so that the picture keeps its (doubled) size. Full screen only uses the aspect ratio: the window stays the size of your screen and the picture is scaled to fit it, with black bars where the shapes differ. The choice is remembered for next time.

The demonstration games (on the title screen and between the credits pages) play in whichever shape you chose, and play out just as they were recorded.

A new resolution takes hold at once during a level, where the picture is redrawn around the camera. On other screens (the title screen, for instance) it takes hold when the next screen starts.

Tip:

The command line option --resolution N starts the game in one of the shapes without changing the saved choice: 0 is the original, 1 is 16:9, 2 is 8:5, 3 is 5:4 and 4 is 4:3.

Full screen

Press F11 or choose File → Fullscreen to switch between a window and full screen. The menu bar and status bar are hidden in full screen; press F11 again to bring them back.

Turn It Up! Audio

The Audio menu holds a switch and a volume slider for the music and another pair for the sound effects, and a Channels submenu that lets you silence individual sound chip channels (the six FM channels, the three PSG tone channels, the noise channel and the DAC, which plays the sampled drums). A channel is audible while its entry is checked; Unmute All turns them all back on. The settings are remembered between runs.

Secrets, Cheats and Easter Eggs

The debug cheat

At the title screen enter C, C, C, C, Up, Down, Left, Right (you hear a ring). The View and Tools menus appear, the console can be opened, and in a level you can press B to leave Sonic's body and fly around placing objects: C places the selected object, A selects the next one, / the previous one and , . (or [ ]) change its subtype; B again turns back into Sonic. Special stages and the ending have object lists of their own.

The same code also switches on the hidden Japanese credits described below.

The Japanese credits

With the debug cheat entered earlier in the session, hold A, B, C and Down together while the title sequence starts with the SONIC TEAM PRESENTS screen: the Japanese staff credits are shown instead.

The Easter Eggs menu

The sound test on the level select has two more numbers after its last sound: 9E and 9F. (In the original game, once a cheat was on, they started the credits and the ending directly.) Step to them with Right: the numbers between the last real sound and 9E are empty slots that play nothing, so keep going. From 00, Left wraps round to 9F in one press. No cheat code is needed.

The sound test at 9E

Press a button on either of them and the Easter Eggs menu opens.

Under the Hood: The Debug Tools

These tools are only available while the debug cheat is active (always, in a debug build). Opening one of them from the View or Tools menu opens a window of its own; the status bar names every tool that is in use.

The View menu

VDP Viewer

The video memory as tiles, the four palettes and the sprites, updated as the game runs.

Sound Viewer

The registers of the YM2612 FM chip and the SN76489 sound chip: key states, algorithms, frequencies, levels and routings.

Console

A drop-down console (the ` key also opens it) for reading and setting the game's variables and running commands. The game is frozen while it is open.

Variables

The game's global variables, which you can edit.

Logging

Starts and stops the event log, chooses its file and shows it in a window.

Object RAM

The object slots: type, routine, position and speed of each.

The Tools menu

Record Demo...

Asks for a level (or a special stage) and records your button presses to a file, with a record of where it started. Stop Recording saves it and carries on playing.

Play Demo...

Plays a recorded demo back in its level.

SMPS Inspector

See .

The SMPS Inspector

The SMPS Inspector shows what the sound engine is doing, as it does it, and lets you play any song or sound effect.

The SMPS Inspector

The game is frozen while the Inspector is open, but the sound engine keeps running, so that nothing from the game can start a sound that competes with the one you chose.

The sound list

Every song and sound effect, by its sound test number and name. Choosing one plays it (<, >, Left and Right step through the list). Stop silences everything.

JSON engine

Plays the sound through the JSON tree-walking engine (the one the level select's sound test uses) instead of the byte-code driver the game itself uses. Comparing the two is how timing differences were found.

The channel strips

A piano keyboard for each FM and PSG tone channel that the song uses, with the note being played lit. A channel that drives the noise generator shows a row of squares instead, since its notes pick the character of the noise.

The drum kit

All ninety-five drums of the ParadoxSMPS drum kit. A drum lights when the song hits it, and clicking one plays it by itself.

The settings file

Your choices (audio switches, volumes and muted channels, full screen, the window's size and position, the picture's shape, the demo folder) are saved in ~/.local/share/SonicPC/Sonic1Settings.cfg, a YAML file that is rewritten as things change. Delete it to start over with the defaults.

Troubleshooting

Q: The picture has black bars.

The window is a different shape from the picture. Resize it, or choose the resolution that matches your screen. Full screen always keeps the picture's shape.

Q: There is no sound.

Check that Audio → Music and Sound Effects are on and that their volumes are up, and that the channels you want are checked under Channels.

Q: The View and Tools menus are missing.

They only appear while the debug cheat is active ().

Q: A new resolution does not seem to take hold.

Outside a level it takes hold when the next screen starts; start a level or return to the title screen.
