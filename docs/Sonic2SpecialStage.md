# Sonic 2 pre-beta special stage: the plan (stages)

The pre-beta's special stage is the largest single piece left for M4: about 2,900 lines of 68000 for the stage itself (`Special_Stage` at $5BB0 to $8C9C in `S2140992.asm`, mostly the track drawing's unrolled loops and tables), the characters' objects ($09 Sonic about 600 lines, $10 Miles about 250, the shadows $63), the objects of the stage ($5E HUD, $5F start flag, $60 rings, $61 bombs, $62 timer, $87 ring counter), the results screen ($7E, shared with Sonic 1's) and 59 blobs of track data (`Data/SS/*.dat`). It is not Sonic 1's rotating maze (which the port uses today, from Sonic 1's `GM_Special`): it is Sonic 2's half-pipe, where the track is drawn as tiles and the characters run towards the screen. It is built in stages, each one tested on its own; nothing is switched over from Sonic 1's stage until the last.

## Stages

1. **Data and art (done 2026-11-12).** `tools/import_s2prebeta_ss.pl` brings in 22 Nemesis pictures (`res/Art/SpecialStage*`: the characters' three frames' sets, the shadows, background, HUD font, traffic lights, rings, bombs, explosion, emerald, the wings and hand), the main palette (`res/S2Palette/SpecialStage`, palette list $18) and the 59 data blobs (`res/SSData/<what it is>`: the track's segments and two others, as the ROM has them). The pre-beta's two lists, $3C (`PlcId_SSMain`) and $3D (`PlcId_SSRings`, the rings over the traffic lights), are in `Sonic2PLC.c`, the tile numbers in `Object/SpecialStageArt.h` (the `ArtTile_SpecialStage*` names). Test: every picture of the two lists decodes to its header's tile count, and the lists go where the pre-beta's do. The leftovers of an earlier build (`PB_*`) are not imported. The names have the prefix `SpecialStage` because Sonic 1's `SSEmerald` and the rest are the other stage's.
2. **The tables (done 2026-11-13).** The `dc` data of the stage in the asm: the stage order and segment index ($6CFC, $6D5A: which segment each step of a stage uses and the segments' addresses, the 0x4 bytes of each segment's header), the objects' layouts ($309C8: where the rings and bombs of each segment are), the stage's own constants ($5EE2, $5F02, $6E3A, $7302, $751A) as C tables and checks of their sizes against the blobs.
3. **The track** (3a done 2026-11-13: the decoder and the order; 3b done 2026-11-13: the display model and a viewer; H32 mode for the engine still to do). The drawing: the decoder that turns a segment's frames into the tiles of plane A (the 3D half-pipe), the scroll and the background (plane B), the palette's cycling. A viewer with no characters: the track runs by itself through the stage's segments, the same picture frame for frame as the pre-beta's (its reference: the user's Kega Fusion captures or the disassembly's step by step).
4. **The characters.** Sonic and Miles (objects $09 and $10): the half-pipe movement (the angle, the speed and the jump), their art streamed frame by frame (`Art_Sonic_In_Special_Stage` and the others, by the angle's frame), the shadows ($63), Tails' tail, and the second player (`true_two_player` and the pads).
5. **The objects of the stage.** The rings ($60) and bombs ($61) coming down the track from the layouts, the hits (the rings counted, a bomb costing rings), the HUD ($5E, the ring counter $87, the timer $62, the start flag and traffic lights $5F).
6. **The flow.** The stage's start (the transition in, the music $92, the traffic lights), its end (the ring counts needed per segment, the emerald), the results screen and the way back to the level, the continues, the emeralds kept, the 2P versions if the pre-beta has any, the entry from the level select and the stage's number.
7. **Switching over.** The pre-beta's stage replaces Sonic 1's (`GM_Special` stays Sonic 1's own), the demo (`Demo_NASS`) and the level select entries are looked at again, a play test.

Each stage's notes are added below as it is done.

## Stage 1 notes (2026-11-12)

* Palette list $18 loads `Pal_Special_Stage_Main` into the line 0 on ($FB00, 32 words): all four lines, as the file has them.
* List $3C puts the emerald at $2E80, the wings and hand at $3440, the background at $E000 (plane B's tiles, which the stage's tile map reads at tile $700), the HUD font at $46E0, the three shadows at $4F20, $53E0 and $5B20, the traffic lights at $61A0 and the bombs' explosion at $7A00; list $3D loads the rings over the traffic lights. Sonic's and Miles' art and the bombs are not in a list: they are loaded by the stage's own code (stage 4 and 5).
* The pre-beta's `Special_Stage` first plays sound $CA (the entry's flash) and sets the VDP to the stage's mode (plane A 64x32? at $C000, plane B at $E000, scroll mode: the registers $8B03, $8004, $8230, $8405, $8C08, $9003, $8700, $8D3F, $857C): stage 3 reads them against the port's VDP.

## Stage 2 notes (2026-11-13)

* `tools/asm_dc_region.pl SOURCE FIRST_LABEL END_LABEL OUTPUT` turns a stretch of the disassembly's `dc.b/w/l` data into its big-endian binary (numbers, differences of labels, and the `Offset_0x…` addresses in a `dc.l`). Five tables became resources: `SSData/SegmentLists` (the 5 lists of segment numbers: a table of offsets and their bytes, 94 bytes), `TileWords` (612 tile words), `RunTiles` (134 pairs of a tile and its repeat count less one), `TrackBlocks` (56 words, one for each segment, to its block of the track's data, and the blocks to $8B9E: 5,764 bytes) and `ObjectLayouts` (the rings' and bombs' layouts of the 13 parts). The segments' own pointer table ($6D5A) is `src/SpecialStageSegments.inc` (56 ROM offsets, the order the stage's numbers use); the 20 bytes of $5F02 (how many segments each list gives, and the 14 steps of the stage's sequence) are in C in `SpecialStageTables.c`.
* **A segment** is three sections, each a 4-byte header (the last word is the length of the data after it): the stage's code at $5F62 takes the second's and third's places from those words. 55 of the 56 files fill their section exactly; `0C853E` has an odd length and a byte of padding.
* **The stage's sequence:** `3,3,3,3,4,0,1,$80,2,$83,4,0,$81,$FF`: a step's low bits name one of the 5 lists, bit 7 is a flag the stage's code reads (stage 3 finds what for), $FF ends it (the code restarts from the first). The lists give 24, 24, 12, 16 and 7 segments (the fifth has a pad byte). The first list is the start ($26 to $2B, $26 again) and then $00 to $10 in order.
* **The rings and bombs layouts** ($309C8): a count less one and then pairs (the distance along the segment with bit 7 for a bomb, and the place across), for 13 parts, 10 of them empty: the sixth has eight (seven rings and a bomb), the seventh two (a ring and a bomb), the tenth nine rings on a line. (A layout's trailing 0 byte is padding.)
* paradoxmake puts 8 zero bytes after every resource: `SpecialStageTables.c` subtracts them (`RES_SIZE`), and a test pins the first segment's real size (1,188).
* `SpecialStageTables.h` is what the later stages read: `SS_Segment`/`SS_SegmentSections`, `SS_List`/`SS_ListLength`/`SS_SequenceStep`, `SS_TileWord`, `SS_RunTile`/`SS_RunRepeat`, `SS_TrackBlock`, `SS_ObjectCount`/`SS_ObjectEntry`. What the track blocks ($751A) hold is read by the characters' code (stage 4), the tile tables by the track's drawing (stage 3): the accessors give their bytes, not yet their meaning.

## Stage 3a notes (2026-11-13): how a segment becomes the track

Found by reading the pre-beta's unrolled decoder ($5FFC to $6678, and its twin at $66AA that writes backwards) and checked by decoding all 56 segments: every one comes out as exactly **28 units of 128 words**, with all three streams used to their end.

* A segment's three sections are three **MSB-first bit streams**: *flags* (one bit a command: 1 a single tile, 0 a run), *tiles* (a bit: 1 a 10-bit number, 0 a 6-bit one; the 10-bit numbers get $40 added; the number indexes the 612 words of $6E3A, and $6000 is ORed on) and *runs* (a bit: 1 a 7-bit number plus $40, 0 a 6-bit one; the number indexes the 134 pairs of $7302: a tile ($E000 ORed on) and how many times it is written, less one). A unit ends at a $FF byte in the runs where a byte begins, or at the 7-bit number $7F. The 68000's unrolled handlers (one for each place in a byte) are only this, read by bit position.
* The words are **name table entries** (palette 3, the priority bit for the runs' tiles). A unit is 4 rows of the plane's 32 cells; a segment is 112 rows. The stage decodes **7 units a frame** (a *slice*) for four frames, to four places of the name table ($8000, $8700, $8E00, $9500, and $C000 and up for the second buffer: the plane's address register ($8220 or $8230) flips between the two when the segment's time is out), keeping the streams' places between the calls and clearing them after the fourth.
* **Flipped track:** the twin decoder writes each unit's 128 words backwards into RAM ($FFFF9000 and on, one $100 bytes a unit), which the VBlank handler copies to the same places in VRAM: each unit turned round (its rows in the other order and each row's cells too). **A single tile's word is XORed with $6800 there instead of ORed with $6000** (the 16 literal handlers: `eori.w #$6800`): the palette goes on the same and the tile is turned the other way across (the h-flip bit, $0800), as the mirrored cells need; a run's tile is ORed with $E000 as in the plain decoder. The table at $6E3A has 372 plain words and 240 h-flipped ones (no v-flip bit anywhere: the tiles are 8 identical rows). `SSDecoder_Slice(…, flipped, …)` does it all.
* **The order** (Offset_0x005F16's first half): the stage's step (the sequence's byte; bit 7 is a flag the turn reads) names a list, the place in the list gives the segment, and at the list's end the next step starts; the step $FF restarts the sequence **with the first list** (the pre-beta's slip: the first step's list is another). A sequence is 226 segments. **Turning** (Offset_0x008B9E, run on the segment chosen): segment $11 always turns the track over; $0D and $1A turn it over when the step's flag is set (by the step the stage is at after the choice went on) and back when it is not; the others leave it as it is.
* `SpecialStageTrack.[ch]`: `SSDecoder` (a segment a slice at a time), `SS_DecodeSegment`, `SSTrack` and `SSTrack_NextSegment`. Tests: every segment decodes to its 28 units (and the flipped one is the units turned round), the slices give the same words as the whole, the 226 segments' order, the turning and the restart.
* **Not yet (3b):** the VDP side. The pre-beta puts these words straight into the name table of plane A in VRAM and flips the plane's address between two buffers, then moves it with a line scroll buffer ($FFFFE000, set up at $8C22 and changed each frame by $112C: the speed makes a segment last 8 to $C frames or so), a second buffer for the flipped case ($FFFF9700), per-line scrolling of the horizon, the palette cycling, and plane B's background; the engine's VDP draws its planes from its own tables, so 3b begins with how a plane of these words is held and drawn (and the 32 by 128 size, and the line scroll).

## Stage 3b notes (2026-11-13): the track on the screen

* **Plane A is 128 cells wide and 32 high** (the stage's register $9003), not 32 by 128: a *row* of it is 128 words, a decoded **unit**, and holds **four 32-cell stripes**; the line scroll table (A and B words for each of 256 lines, built at $8C22: A = 0, -$100, -$200, -$300 for two lines each, over and over; the buffer for the turned track at $FFFF9700 has them the other way round) shows each 2-pixel band of a tile row from its own stripe. A segment is 28 rows: 224 lines. The tiles are 8 identical rows (the table at $DA672: 372 rows of 8 pixels, written to VRAM $0000 at $8C6C by repeating each 8 times), so the whole picture is made by the bands. The two buffers are at $8000 and $C000 (the plane address register is $8220 and $8230); plane B is at $A000 with the background's Enigma map ($DCBCA, tiles from $700) four times across.
* **The frame** (`SpecialStageDisplay.[ch]`, the RAM of $FFFF9B00 on as `SSDisplay`): the blank first (`SSDisplay_VBlank`: the pace, Offset_0x00112C, then the frame of the segment's time goes on and at its end the plane is changed to the buffer decoded and the next is decoded into the other), then the main loop (`SSDisplay_Decode`: a new segment on frame 0, a slice a frame on frames 0 to 3, nothing on later frames but the turning check; `SSDisplay_Scroll`: the background's X and Y moved by the curves). The **pace** gives how many frames each segment lasts from the stage's speed word: 12 patterns of frame counts ($11B8, extracted as `SSData/PacePatterns`), the pattern as long as twice the speed (plus one for a half fraction): speed 12 is 5 frames a segment, 8 is 8 and 7 by turns, 15 is 4. The **curves** ($8E5C vertical, $8FEC horizontal: `SSData/VerticalCurves` and `HorizontalCurves`) are picked by the list and the place in the list, each with 5 entries for the segment's 4 to 8 frames; a step is the entry's high byte and the sine of its low byte, shifted 8 bits, added or subtracted (turned: the other way). The pre-beta compares `D1` where it means `D2` in the vertical curves of list 1 ($8E10), so from the 19th segment of that list the curve is always the same one: kept.
* **The start** (`SSDisplay_Prime`, $5D8A to $5DE2): the first period passes with nothing decoded; the first segment ($11) is decoded into the second buffer during the second and shown from its end, with the second already begun in the first.
* **The viewer** (`GM_SpecialTrack.c`): the special stage's screen with `SONIC2_SS_TRACK=1` in the environment shows the track with no characters (up and down change the speed, right the half step, Start leaves). The palette is the stage's ($18: `PalId_SpecialStage2`), the art from list $3C. Headless it can be seen with `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen SONIC2_SS_TRACK=1 SONIC_HASH=<file> SONIC_DUMP="<frame>:<prefix>" Sonic2 --special 0` (8 frames of PPM from that frame).
* **Known:** the stage runs in the 32-cell-wide mode (register $8C08: 256 pixels); the engine draws 320 (or wider), so the stripes repeat at the sides of the picture. The engine needs a narrow mode (draw the middle 256 and leave the sides the backdrop) before the stage is right; until then the viewer shows the picture as it is. The palette cycling of the track and the music's start are not done (stage 6).

## Stage 3b, the viewer and widescreen (2026-11-13)

* **What the blockiness was:** not the interlaced/tall mode. The game's VBlank uploads its own `hscroll_buffer` and `vid_*_scrpos_y_dup` every frame, which had overwritten the viewer's line scroll table: every tile row showed one stripe instead of four 2-pixel bands. The viewer now writes the game's own buffer, and the picture is smooth (the image the user showed). The stage needed no interlace mode.
* **Widescreen** (`SpecialStageWide.[ch]`, the user's choice): the pre-beta's picture is 256 pixels (32-cell mode); on a wider screen the track stays pixel-exact in the middle 256, **each line's edge pixel is carried 8 pixels out at both sides** (the cell next to the track is a tile of that colour: 16 solid tiles at `ArtTile_SpecialStageSolid`, so the walls look solid and the picture has depth), the **rest of the sides is cropped** (blank cells: the background shows whole), and the background (plane B) is drawn the full width. The plane is made wider: a row is 4 stripes each with margins (256 cells across for screens up to 496 pixels, 512 beyond), and the line scroll puts each stripe in the middle (`SSWide_ScrollA`, the background's `SSWide_ScrollB` adds the same offset). The scratch plane memory's plane size limit was raised to $4000 entries (`Viewport.c`). The screen's height is not touched (a taller screen shows the track's 224 lines at the top; to do with the characters). The characters' and objects' X positions will need the screen's offset (`SSWide.x0`) when stage 4 draws them.
* **The background's bottom half:** the background is two Enigma maps, 32 by 16 cells each ($DCBCA the top and $DCA9C the bottom, decoded to RAM at $FFFF0000 and $FFFF0400 and drawn as one 32 by 32 block at $8C9C): the first version decoded only the top, and the bottom showed garbage. Test: every cell names one of the background's 127 tiles.

## Stage 4a notes (2026-11-13): Sonic on the pipe

* **The character** (`SpecialStageCharacter.[ch]`, `SSPlayer`; Obj $09 and the pieces it calls, Offset_0x02ED2C to Offset_0x02F430): it runs round a half pipe on an **angle** (a byte: $40 the bottom, 1 and $7F the sides) at a speed called *inertia*. The pad adds or takes a step to the inertia (the speed of the stage, 8 to $C, chooses it: $100, $180, $280, $480, $A80), and lets it run down by 8 a frame when neither left nor right is held; the pipe's slope adds a pull to the bottom that grows with the distance from it (the table of $41 words at $2F36E: $0 to $2000), the inertia is held to the fastest the speed allows ($2000 to $4400), and past the sides ($80 and over, 0) the character either goes round to the other side (slowly) or can no longer be steered (inertia $2000 or more). The angle goes on by the inertia's top 4 bits and the place is the ellipse: X = cos × $50 and Y = sin × $64 about ($7F, $44). A button (A, B or C) jumps: the speed ($680 a frame) is along the angle's opposite; in the air the angle is made from how far across the character is ($2EFEC) and it is back on the pipe when the sum of its distances to two points ($3C above and below the middle) is $C8 or more (the pre-beta's Newton square root gives 0 when its ten iterations are not enough: the far one often is, and that is kept).
* **The pictures** (`Object/SpecialStageObjects.[ch]`, `Obj_SpecialStageSonic` and `Obj_SpecialStageShadow`): Sonic's four sets of art (the plain, diagonal, horizontal and spin ones, Nemesis) are decoded at the start; each frame's tiles are copied to VRAM tile $2F2 as its script says (`SSData/CharacterArtScript`, shared with Miles; Offset_0x02EED4): a count of words and each word the number of tiles (4 bits, less one) and where they begin (12 bits, in words); the mapping's tiles are numbered from there. The animation (`Animation/SpecialStageSonic`) is the pre-beta's own routine (Offset_0x02F1E4): each frame lasts as many frames as the track's segment does, and the running animation turns Sonic round every 4 steps. The **shadow** ($63) is under Sonic on the pipe and, in the air, where the angle puts it on the pipe; it has 8 looks by the angle (the player's, the diagonal and the vertical shadow's art at $279, $29F and $2D9).
* **The mappings** must be listed under `mappings_SonicTheHedgehog2:` (the build converts Sonic 2's piece format): in `resources:` they are copied as bytes and the sprites come out as rubbish.
* **The viewer** (`SONIC2_SS_TRACK=1`) now runs Sonic: left and right turn him round the pipe, A, B or C jump, Up and Down still change the speed. The sprites are placed with the camera at $-x0$, which is how the 256-pixel stage's places become the middle of a wider screen.
* **Not yet:** rings and bombs touching Sonic (stage 5), Miles and his tails (4b), the hit tumble's ring scatter, and Tails' own pad.

**Names of the data (2026-11-13):** the files of `res/SSData` are named for what they are, not by the ROM offsets the disassembly's labels have: `Segment00` to `Segment37` (the stage's segments in the order of its table at $6D5A; `SpecialStageSegments.inc`), `SegmentLists` ($6CFC), `TileWords` ($6E3A), `RunTiles` ($7302), `TrackBlocks` ($751A), `ObjectLayouts` ($309C8), `PacePatterns` ($11B8), `VerticalCurves` ($8E5C), `HorizontalCurves` ($8FEC), `CharacterArtScript` ($2F94E), `TrackTiles` ($DA672), `BackgroundMapTop` ($DCBCA) and `BackgroundMapBottom` ($DCA9C). The importer (`tools/import_s2prebeta_ss.pl`) has the names.

## Stage 4b notes (2026-11-13): Miles and his tails

* **Miles** (Obj $10, `SSPlayer_InitMiles`, `Obj_SpecialStageMiles`): the same movement as Sonic's with other numbers: he starts at ($7F,$B8) with the middle of his pipe $80 above, $66 across and $80 down (Sonic's $50 and $64), a priority in front of Sonic's, art at tile $381 in palette 2, and his landing ellipse has its points $4D above and below the middle with $100 for the two distances (Sonic's $3C and $C8). He is the second character's slot (Player_2) and has a shadow of his own ($63).
* **His pad** (Offset_0x02FD5C): when pad 2's buttons are worked (any of up, down, left, right, B, C, A) he does what they say, the history of Sonic's pad is wiped and he goes on doing what they say for 3 seconds ($B4 frames) after; then he does **what Sonic's pad did 15 frames ago** (the history of Sonic's pad at $FFFF9B62 to $FFFF9B80 goes a place each frame, newest first: Offset_0x02ED44). `true_two_player` and the network will give pad 2 from the other player; nothing in the stage is changed for that.
* **His art:** the stage's script ($2F94E, `CharacterArtScript`) has Sonic's frames from entry 0, **Miles' from entry $32** (a count and words, as Sonic's) and **his tails' from entry $44, a single word each** (no count), copied to VRAM tile $375. The sets of the art (plain, diagonal, horizontal, spin; the tails' plain, diagonal and horizontal) are chosen by the frame as Sonic's are (the tails' at 7 and $E).
* **His tails** (Obj $88, `Obj_SpecialStageMilesTail`): in Miles' place with his flips and animation; while he runs (the first three animations) they have an animation of their own (3 frames each); while he jumps (the third) they are gone. Priority one in front less than his.
* Tests: Miles' place, pipe and landing ellipse and his running and jumping. The viewer shows Sonic behind and Miles in front with his tails.

The viewer loads a layout on the 4th frame of a segment and loads list $3D (rings) after the start.

## Stage 5c: the rings a bomb knocks out ($5B)

* On the first step of a tumble with rings (`SSPlayer.spill`, set in `Tumble`) the object that draws the character spawns up to 10 `Obj_SpecialStageRingOut` rings and takes ten off the count (with ten or fewer all go; an odd count uses the second angle table). Each flies at `(angle + $40 + table)` (x speed cos*$400, y speed sin*$800), falls with gravity, and goes in front (frame 9, priority 0) on its way down; gone off the 256-pixel stage's sides or below $E0.
* Tested logic only (first step spills once; no rings, nothing); not yet seen on screen.

## Stages 6 and 7: the flow, and the switch

What the pre-beta's stage does (read from `Special_Stage`, Offset_0x005BB0): it sets up, shows the START banner (no one is held back: only the clock waits for the banner), and then loops for ever. **It has no end, no results screen, no emerald and no palette cycling**: the time reaching 00 does nothing, and the only ways out are the demo timer and, with the slow-motion cheat, A while paused (to the title). Those parts (emeralds, results, a time-up) belong to later milestones, not to 1:1 with the pre-beta.

* `Screen_Special` now runs the pre-beta's stage (`GM_SpecialTrack`). Start pauses, B/C step a frame, A leaves for the title (the shared `PauseGame`). `SONIC2_SS_SONIC1=1` still gives Sonic 1's stage; `SONIC2_SS_TRACK=1` the old viewer controls (Up/Down speed, Start leaves).
* The speed is the stage's fixed $C (the pre-beta never changes it).
* Not done: the stage's demo (`Init_Demo_Control`), the H32 narrow mode on a 256-pixel screen, the taller-than-224 screen, and the play test by the user.

### Palette bug fixed (the "black where white should be")

Sonic 1's special stage palette cycle (`PCycle_SS`, run by the shared blank for routine $0A) was overwriting colours 7-12 of line 3 and 7-10 of line 4 of the stage's palette with Sonic 1's cycle. That cycle took the font's and the track's white. `ss_sonic1_palette_cycle` (PaletteCycle.c) turns it off for the pre-beta's stage; `Screen_Special` sets it only for Sonic 1's.

Open: in the Sonic Retro screenshot of the pre-beta stage the track's lanes are blue, in ours yellow/brown. `SS_Main.pal` has the blues on line 2 (colours 3-7) and the lanes' yellow on line 3 (colours 3-7), so either the pre-beta cycles a line at run time (I found no cycle in its blank, routine $0A) or the tile words' palette lines differ. To compare against the pre-beta in an emulator.

### Palette, shadows and the shadow/highlight mode (from the Kega dumps, 2026-11-13)

Checked against the user's VRAM/CRAM dumps and a Kega screenshot of the pre-beta (`s2built_VRAM.ram`, `s2built_CRAM.ram` in the disassembly folder; both are byte-swapped words):
* **The stage runs in shadow/highlight mode** (`$8C08`). The shadows of the characters and items are colour 15 of palette line 3 (the shadow operator), not a colour: `GM_SpecialTrack` now turns the mode on (and off at the end).
* **Every tile of the track has its priority bit** (also the transparent ones), so the background is never shadowed. The engine's VDP now keeps this: a plane tile with the priority bit counts as normal intensity even where its pixel is transparent (`VDP_MASK_SH_NORMAL`, and in `vdp.comp`). The widescreen margins' blank tile carries the bit too.
* **Operators obey sprite order and priority**: a shadow/highlight operator pixel does nothing under an earlier sprite or a priority tile (it darkened the characters in front of it before).
* **Colour 7 of line 3 is $0EC2 (cyan) after the fade**, where the file has $0F00: the pre-beta's `Pal_DecRed_2` takes 2 off the red every frame without comparing it with the target, so a colour that is not a valid Mega Drive colour ends where 22 frames of the fade leave it. `WhiteIn_DecColour` (engine) now does the same; the track's edges are cyan.
* Sonic 1's palette cycle is off in this stage (above). The real CRAM has no cycling: lines match `SS_Main.pal` except colour 7 and the lights' colours 11-13.
