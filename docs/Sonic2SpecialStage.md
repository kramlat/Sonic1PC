# Sonic 2 pre-beta special stage: the plan (stages)

The pre-beta's special stage is the largest single piece left for M4: about 2,900 lines of 68000 for the stage itself (`Special_Stage` at $5BB0 to $8C9C in `S2140992.asm`, mostly the track drawing's unrolled loops and tables), the characters' objects ($09 Sonic about 600 lines, $10 Miles about 250, the shadows $63), the objects of the stage ($5E HUD, $5F start flag, $60 rings, $61 bombs, $62 timer, $87 ring counter), the results screen ($7E, shared with Sonic 1's) and 59 blobs of track data (`Data/SS/*.dat`). It is not Sonic 1's rotating maze (which the port uses today, from Sonic 1's `GM_Special`): it is Sonic 2's half-pipe, where the track is drawn as tiles and the characters run towards the screen. It is built in stages, each one tested on its own; nothing is switched over from Sonic 1's stage until the last.

## Stages

1. **Data and art (done 2026-11-12).** `tools/import_s2prebeta_ss.pl` brings in 22 Nemesis pictures (`res/Art/SpecialStage*`: the characters' three frames' sets, the shadows, background, HUD font, traffic lights, rings, bombs, explosion, emerald, the wings and hand), the main palette (`res/S2Palette/SpecialStage`, palette list $18) and the 59 data blobs (`res/SSData/<ROM offset>`: the track's segments and two others, as the ROM has them). The pre-beta's two lists, $3C (`PlcId_SSMain`) and $3D (`PlcId_SSRings`, the rings over the traffic lights), are in `Sonic2PLC.c`, the tile numbers in `Object/SpecialStageArt.h` (the `ArtTile_SpecialStage*` names). Test: every picture of the two lists decodes to its header's tile count, and the lists go where the pre-beta's do. The leftovers of an earlier build (`PB_*`) are not imported. The names have the prefix `SpecialStage` because Sonic 1's `SSEmerald` and the rest are the other stage's.
2. **The tables (done 2026-11-13).** The `dc` data of the stage in the asm: the stage order and segment index ($6CFC, $6D5A: which segment each step of a stage uses and the segments' addresses, the 0x4 bytes of each segment's header), the objects' layouts ($309C8: where the rings and bombs of each segment are), the stage's own constants ($5EE2, $5F02, $6E3A, $7302, $751A) as C tables and checks of their sizes against the blobs.
3. **The track** (3a done 2026-11-13: the decoder and the order; 3b, the drawing on screen, next). The drawing: the decoder that turns a segment's frames into the tiles of plane A (the 3D half-pipe), the scroll and the background (plane B), the palette's cycling. A viewer with no characters: the track runs by itself through the stage's segments, the same picture frame for frame as the pre-beta's (its reference: the user's Kega Fusion captures or the disassembly's step by step).
4. **The characters.** Sonic and Miles (objects $09 and $10): the half-pipe movement (the angle, the speed and the jump), their art streamed frame by frame (`Art_Sonic_In_Special_Stage` and the others, by the angle's frame), the shadows ($63), Tails' tail, and the second player (`true_two_player` and the pads).
5. **The objects of the stage.** The rings ($60) and bombs ($61) coming down the track from the layouts, the hits (the rings counted, a bomb costing rings), the HUD ($5E, the ring counter $87, the timer $62, the start flag and traffic lights $5F).
6. **The flow.** The stage's start (the transition in, the music $92, the traffic lights), its end (the ring counts needed per segment, the emerald), the results screen and the way back to the level, the continues, the emeralds kept, the 2P versions if the pre-beta has any, the entry from the level select and the stage's number.
7. **Switching over.** The pre-beta's stage replaces Sonic 1's (`GM_Special` stays Sonic 1's own), the demo (`Demo_NASS`) and the level select entries are looked at again, a play test.

Each stage's notes are added below as it is done.

## Stage 1 notes (2026-11-12)

* Palette list $18 loads `Pal_Special_Stage_Main` into the line 0 on ($FB00, 32 words): all four lines, as the file has them.
* List $3C puts the emerald at $2E80, the wings and hand at $3440, the background at $E000 (plane B's tiles, which the stage's tile map reads at tile $700), the HUD font at $46E0, the three shadows at $4F20, $53E0 and $5B20, the traffic lights at $61A0 and the bombs' explosion at $7A00; list $3D loads the rings over the traffic lights. Sonic's and Miles' art and the bombs are not in a list: they are loaded by the stage's own code (stage 4 and 5).
* The pre-beta's `Special_Stage` first plays sound $CA (the entry's flash) and sets the VDP to the stage's mode (plane A 64x32? at $C000, plane B at $E000, scroll mode: the registers $8B03, $8004, $8230, $8405, $8C08, $9003, $8700, $8D3F, $857C): stage 3 reads them against the port's VDP.

## Stage 2 notes (2026-11-13)

* `tools/asm_dc_region.pl SOURCE FIRST_LABEL END_LABEL OUTPUT` turns a stretch of the disassembly's `dc.b/w/l` data into its big-endian binary (numbers, differences of labels, and the `Offset_0x…` addresses in a `dc.l`). Five tables became resources: `SSData/006CFC` (the 5 lists of segment numbers: a table of offsets and their bytes, 94 bytes), `006E3A` (612 tile words), `007302` (134 pairs of a tile and its repeat count less one), `00751A` (56 words, one for each segment, to its block of the track's data, and the blocks to $8B9E: 5,764 bytes) and `0309C8` (the rings' and bombs' layouts of the 13 parts). The segments' own pointer table ($6D5A) is `src/SpecialStageSegments.inc` (56 ROM offsets, the order the stage's numbers use); the 20 bytes of $5F02 (how many segments each list gives, and the 14 steps of the stage's sequence) are in C in `SpecialStageTables.c`.
* **A segment** is three sections, each a 4-byte header (the last word is the length of the data after it): the stage's code at $5F62 takes the second's and third's places from those words. 55 of the 56 files fill their section exactly; `0C853E` has an odd length and a byte of padding.
* **The stage's sequence:** `3,3,3,3,4,0,1,$80,2,$83,4,0,$81,$FF`: a step's low bits name one of the 5 lists, bit 7 is a flag the stage's code reads (stage 3 finds what for), $FF ends it (the code restarts from the first). The lists give 24, 24, 12, 16 and 7 segments (the fifth has a pad byte). The first list is the start ($26 to $2B, $26 again) and then $00 to $10 in order.
* **The rings and bombs layouts** ($309C8): a count less one and then pairs (the distance along the segment with bit 7 for a bomb, and the place across), for 13 parts, 10 of them empty: the sixth has eight (seven rings and a bomb), the seventh two (a ring and a bomb), the tenth nine rings on a line. (A layout's trailing 0 byte is padding.)
* paradoxmake puts 8 zero bytes after every resource: `SpecialStageTables.c` subtracts them (`RES_SIZE`), and a test pins the first segment's real size (1,188).
* `SpecialStageTables.h` is what the later stages read: `SS_Segment`/`SS_SegmentSections`, `SS_List`/`SS_ListLength`/`SS_SequenceStep`, `SS_TileWord`, `SS_RunTile`/`SS_RunRepeat`, `SS_TrackBlock`, `SS_ObjectCount`/`SS_ObjectEntry`. What the track blocks ($751A) hold is read by the characters' code (stage 4), the tile tables by the track's drawing (stage 3): the accessors give their bytes, not yet their meaning.

## Stage 3a notes (2026-11-13): how a segment becomes the track

Found by reading the pre-beta's unrolled decoder ($5FFC to $6678, and its twin at $66AA that writes backwards) and checked by decoding all 56 segments: every one comes out as exactly **28 units of 128 words**, with all three streams used to their end.

* A segment's three sections are three **MSB-first bit streams**: *flags* (one bit a command: 1 a single tile, 0 a run), *tiles* (a bit: 1 a 10-bit number, 0 a 6-bit one; the 10-bit numbers get $40 added; the number indexes the 612 words of $6E3A, and $6000 is ORed on) and *runs* (a bit: 1 a 7-bit number plus $40, 0 a 6-bit one; the number indexes the 134 pairs of $7302: a tile ($E000 ORed on) and how many times it is written, less one). A unit ends at a $FF byte in the runs where a byte begins, or at the 7-bit number $7F. The 68000's unrolled handlers (one for each place in a byte) are only this, read by bit position.
* The words are **name table entries** (palette 3, the priority bit for the runs' tiles). A unit is 4 rows of the plane's 32 cells; a segment is 112 rows. The stage decodes **7 units a frame** (a *slice*) for four frames, to four places of the name table ($8000, $8700, $8E00, $9500, and $C000 and up for the second buffer: the plane's address register ($8220 or $8230) flips between the two when the segment's time is out), keeping the streams' places between the calls and clearing them after the fourth.
* **Flipped track:** the twin decoder writes each unit's 128 words backwards into RAM ($FFFF9000 and on, one $100 bytes a unit), which the VBlank handler copies to the same places in VRAM. That is each unit turned round (its rows in the other order and each row's cells too); the tile words themselves are not changed. `SSDecoder_Slice(…, flipped, …)` does it.
* **The order** (Offset_0x005F16's first half): the stage's step (the sequence's byte; bit 7 is a flag the turn reads) names a list, the place in the list gives the segment, and at the list's end the next step starts; the step $FF restarts the sequence **with the first list** (the pre-beta's slip: the first step's list is another). A sequence is 226 segments. **Turning** (Offset_0x008B9E, run on the segment chosen): segment $11 always turns the track over; $0D and $1A turn it over when the step's flag is set (by the step the stage is at after the choice went on) and back when it is not; the others leave it as it is.
* `SpecialStageTrack.[ch]`: `SSDecoder` (a segment a slice at a time), `SS_DecodeSegment`, `SSTrack` and `SSTrack_NextSegment`. Tests: every segment decodes to its 28 units (and the flipped one is the units turned round), the slices give the same words as the whole, the 226 segments' order, the turning and the restart.
* **Not yet (3b):** the VDP side. The pre-beta puts these words straight into the name table of plane A in VRAM and flips the plane's address between two buffers, then moves it with a line scroll buffer ($FFFFE000, set up at $8C22 and changed each frame by $112C: the speed makes a segment last 8 to $C frames or so), a second buffer for the flipped case ($FFFF9700), per-line scrolling of the horizon, the palette cycling, and plane B's background; the engine's VDP draws its planes from its own tables, so 3b begins with how a plane of these words is held and drawn (and the 32 by 128 size, and the line scroll).
